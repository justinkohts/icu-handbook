## Referrals Registrar Role

The **referrals registrar** is responsible for:
- Receiving & assessing referrals to ICU
- Attending:
  - Trauma calls
  - Medical emergency calls
  - Cardiac arrest calls


## Referrals

Avoid informal "just to let you know" calls. If another team has concerns significant enough to contact ICU, it should be clear why they are requesting an ICU review. Ambiguous referrals can create uncertainty about the responsibilities of both the referring and ICU teams. There have been medicolegal and coronial findings both [locally](https://www.hdc.org.nz/decisions/search-decisions/2002/00hdc10145/) and [abroad](https://www.judiciary.uk/prevention-of-future-death-reports/michael-nye-prevention-of-future-deaths-report/) which have highlighted the risks associated with informal or poorly defined referrals. 

Where there is doubt:
- Clarify what the referring team is requesting of ICU.
- If there is a reasonable concern that the patient may require ICU input, treat the referral as a **formal request for review**.
- Clearly document the referral, assessment, advice given, and agreed plan.


## Conducting an ICU Review

An ICU review should be a **complete clinical assessment**, rather than simply determining whether the patient requires ICU admission.

This should include:
- Relevant history, examination, investigations, and physiological trends.
- Working diagnosis and differential diagnosis.
- Assessment of the severity and trajectory of illness.
- Immediate management, monitoring, and further investigations.
- Assessment of whether ICU admission or other organ support is required.


## Clarify the Referring Team's Concerns

Discuss the patient with the **referring team** and establish:
- What has prompted the referral and any specific concerns.
- What deterioration or problems they anticipate.
- What they are asking ICU to provide.
- Their current treatment plan.
- The patient's goals and limits of treatment.

A **[Shared Goals of Care](https://www.myacp.org.nz/what-are-shared-goals-of-care) discussion** should ideally have occurred before ICU referral. If this has not occurred, it may need to be addressed during the ICU review, particularly when escalation to intensive care is being considered.


## Discussing the Patient With the Intensivist

The information obtained during your assessment is crucial for the Intensivist to make an informed decision about ICU management or admission.

In most situations:
1. Review the patient first.
2. Formulate your assessment and management plan.
3. Discuss the case with the Intensivist.

Contact the Intensivist **earlier** if the patient is unstable, urgent ICU intervention is likely, the situation is complex, or there is uncertainty about goals of care. The Intensivist may elect to review the patient with you.


## Training Value

Reviewing referrals are an important component of ICU training. It develops skills in assessment, triage, clinical decision-making, communication, teamworking, and presentation.

Registrars should therefore aim to assess and formulate a plan independently before senior discussion whenever the clinical situation permits.


## Documentation
Clearly document the assessment, discussions, decision, rationale, and agreed management plan in the patient notes.

This is particularly important when ICU admission is not considered appropriate or beneficial. Ensure that the patient's [Shared Goals of Care](https://www.myacp.org.nz/what-are-shared-goals-of-care) documentation is updated, so that treatment limitations are clear and unnecessary repeat referrals to the ICU can be avoided.
