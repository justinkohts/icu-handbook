## Overview
Therapeutic anticoagulation is generally indicated for the treatment or prevention of clinically significant thrombosis. In critically ill patients, choice of anticoagulant depends on the indication, bleeding risk, renal function, and anticipated procedures.

LMWH is generally preferred for stable patients with acute VTE, while intravenous UFH may be preferable when rapid interruption or reversal is required.

## Indications
- **Venous thromboembolism:** Confirmed DVT or PE, or those with high clinical suspicion of such.
- **Atrial fibrillation:** Stroke prevention in selected patients according to thromboembolic and bleeding risk.
- **Mechanical prosthetic heart valves:** Long-term warfarin; with perioperative heparin bridging.
- **Acute coronary syndromes:** Usually LMWH or UFH.
- **Other:** Select intracardiac thrombi, mechanical circulatory support, and other specialist indications.

Anticoagulation of a CRRT circuit is a separate indication and does not necessarily provide systemic therapeutic anticoagulation.


## Choice of Anticoagulant

| Agent | Advantages | Limitations |
|:---:|---|---|
| **LMWH (enoxaparin)** | Predictable effect<br>SC administration<br>No routine monitoring (anti-factor Xa in certain cases) | Renal accumulation<br>Longer duration<br>Partially reversible by protamine |
| **Unfractionated Heparin (IV)** | Short half-life<br>Titratable<br>Fully reversible with protamine | Requires monitoring (aPTT)<br>Variable response<br>Higher HIT risk |
| **DOACs** | Oral administration<br>No routine monitoring | Absorption concerns<br>Drug interactions<br>Renal accumulation |
| **Warfarin** | Suitable for mechanical valves<br>Oral administration | Delayed onset<br>INR monitoring<br>Multiple interactions |



### Enoxaparin
#### Dosing
- **Standard treatment dose:** 1 mg/kg SC every 12 hours, based on actual body weight.
- **Alternative:** 1.5 mg/kg SC once daily in selected patients.
- **Renal impairment:**
  - Dose adjustment (usually half normal dose) required when CrCl <30 mL/min.
  - A loading dose should still be given initially to reach therapeutic levels.
  - IV UFH may be preferable in severe or worsening renal failure.
- **Extremes of body weight:**

| Patient group | VTE Prophylaxis | VTE Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Low body weight**<br>(BMI <18 kg/m² and/or TBW <50 kg) | Enoxaparin 30 mg SC daily | Standard weight-based dose: 1 mg/kg SC q12h |
| **High body weight** | BMI ≥40 kg/m²: 40 mg SC q12h<br>BMI ≥50 kg/m²: Consider 60 mg SC q12h | BMI ≥40 kg/m²: 0.7–0.8 mg/kg TBW SC q12h<br>Consider max 150 mg per dose<br>Avoid once-daily dosing |
| **Anti-Xa monitoring** | Minimal clinical utility | Consider on a patient-specific basis |


#### Monitoring
The [**anti-Xa assay**](https://lab.waikatodhb.health.nz/test-guide/view/427/anti-10a-assay) can be used to monitor enoxaparin's anticoagulant effefct. Routine monitoring is not required due to its predictable pharmacokinetics.

Anti-Xa monitoring may be considered in:
- Weight < 50 kg or > 150 kg.
- Severe renal impairment (CrCl < 30 mL/min).
- Patients on renal replacement therapies.
- Specific concerns regarding bleeding risk or drug accumulation.

Consider **peak anti-Xa levels** to monitor efficacy.
- Peak sample approximately 4 hours after SC administration, usually after the 3rd or 4th dose (five half-lives).
- Peak **prophylactic** range: 0.2–0.6 IU/mL.
- Peak **therapeutic** ranges:
  - Once daily dosing: 1.0–2.0 IU/mL.
  - Twice daily dosing: 0.6–1.0 IU/mL.


### Unfractionated Heparin (UFH) Infusion
Consider IV UFH when:
- Rapid interruption or reversal may be required (e.g., anticipated surgery or invasive procedures).
- Severe/worsening renal impairment, which makes LMWH unsuitable.
- Titratable infusion required for a specific indication.

#### Dosing
- See the guideline (document ID 2130) for full dosing details and infusion rate adjustment.
- **Loading dose:**
  - Seek specialist advice on whether a loading dose is required, if there is a high risk of bleeding or has been on recent anticoagulants.
  - ACS: 60 units/kg (max 5000 units).
  - VTE & others: 80 units/kg (up to max 9600 units).
- **Initial infusion rate:**
  - ACS: 12 units/kg/h (max 1000 units/h).
  - VTE & others: 18 units/kg/h.
> ⚠️ Obese patients (BMI ≥ 40 kg/m²) should be initially dosed according to **adjusted body weight**.
- **Target aPTT:**
  - Most indications → 60–100 seconds.
  - Post-thrombolysis → 50–70 seconds to reduce bleeding risk.
- **Infusion rate adjustment:**
![UFH titration](/images/systems/hematology-and-coagulation/ufh_titration.png)



#### Monitoring
Follow guideline (document ID 2130).
- Check aPTT:
  - 6 h after commencing infusion.
  - 6 h after any infusion rate change.
  - Daily once therapeutic range achieved for 2 consecutive readings.
- Monitor platelet count daily for HIT.
- Monitor for signs/symptoms of bleeding.  

## Additional Clinical Considerations
### Contraindications to Therapeutic Anticoagulation
Active major bleeding, recent high-risk intracranial hemorrhage, and severe hemostatic abnormalities may exclude anticoagulation. In acute proximal DVT or PE with an absolute anticoagulation contraindication, consider a retrievable IVC filter.

### Atrial Fibrillation
**Therapeutic LMWH/UFH is not routinely required** when initiating oral anticoagulation for AFib. **Routine bridging is also not recommended** for most ICU AFib patients who are normally maintained on a DOAC or warfarin and have temporary interruption for a procedure, bleeding, or inability to take enteral medication. 

**Bridging** with therapeutic UFH or LMWH **may be appropriate** in mechanical heart valves (especially MV), recent stroke/TIA, or other very high thrombotic risk situations.
