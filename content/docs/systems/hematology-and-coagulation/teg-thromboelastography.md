## Overview
Thromboelastography (TEG) is a viscoelastic assay to measure whole blood clotting initiation, formation, strength, and lysis. Waikato Hospital uses the **TEG6s device**, primarily in cardiothoracic surgery and the immediate post-operative period. **TEG-guided transfusion algorithms** reduce blood product utilization, particularly in cardiac surgery.


## When to Use

TEG is used to assess coagulopathy and guide targeted hemostatic therapy, particularly in patients with **major bleeding**.

- **Cardiac surgery:** Intraoperative or postoperative bleeding.
- **Major trauma:** Hemorrhagic shock requiring massive transfusion, trauma-induced coagulopathy.
- **Obstetric hemorrhage:** Major postpartum hemorrhage.
- **Liver transplantation:** Intraoperative bleeding and coagulopathy.
- **Other major bleeding:** Consider when conventional coagulation tests are insufficient to guide treatment.

> ⚠️ TEG is not routinely indicated to predict bleeding risk, monitor anticoagulation, or risk assess thrombosis.

## How to Use

### TEG Measurement & Interpretation
Results are available through Waikato Hospital TEG Manager following sample processing. **Interpretation is dependent on the assay**, as the TEG6s cartridge provides additional measurements including functional fibrinogen and heparinase. Thus, **utilize the TEG algorithms** when interpreting TEG.

The main principles of interpretation are listed below.

| Parameter | Assessment | Abnormality | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **R time** | Clot initiation | Prolonged: factor deficiency or anticoagulant effect | FFP / Beriplex<br>Reverse anticoagulation if indicated |
| **K time / α angle** | Clot formation speed | Prolonged K / reduced angle: impaired fibrin formation | Cryoprecipitate / fibrinogen concentrate (RiaSTAP) |
| **MA** | Maximum clot strength | Reduced: platelet and/or fibrinogen abnormality | Platelets ± cryoprecipitate |
| **LY30** | Clot breakdown | Increased: hyperfibrinolysis | Tranexamic acid |

## Limitations
- **Incomplete hemostasis assessment:** Does not assess endothelial function, von Willebrand factor activity, or platelet adhesion.
- **Platelet function assessment:** Measures platelet contribution to clot strength, but is insensitive to some qualitative platelet defects and antiplatelet medication effects. Use dedicated platelet function assays for this purpose.
- **Anticoagulant detection:** Cannot reliably exclude or quantify DOAC activity.
- **Predictive ability:** Normal TEG does not reliably exclude bleeding risk, and hypercoagulable results do not reliably predict thrombosis.
