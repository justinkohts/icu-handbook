## Red Blood Cells
- Concentrated (packed) red blood cells.
- Used in hemorrhagic shock or symptomatic anemia.
- **Usual dose: 1 adult unit (~220–340 mL)** → typically increases Hb by ~10 g/L.
- **Transfusion *thresholds* (g/L):**
  - **< 70** for most cases.
  - **< 80** for CABG patients or those with active MI.
  - **< 90** for neurocritical care.
- **Considerations:**
  - ABO/RhD compatibility mandatory.
  - Cross-matching required unless emergency-release (O negative) blood used.
  - Transfuse 1 adult unit at a time with reassessment in stable non-bleeding patients.

## Platelets
- Platelet concentrate, obtained by aphresis or pooled donations.
- Used in thrombocytopenia or platelet dysfunction.
- **Usual dose: 1 adult unit (~200–350 mL)** → typically increases platelet count by ~20 × 10⁹/L.
- **Transfusion targets (× 10⁹/L):**
  - **\> 10** if no risk factors.
  - **\> 20** for CVC insertion
  - **\> 40** for lumbar puncture.
  - **\> 50** for major surgery, major bleeding, or high risk procedures.
  - **\> 75** for massive hemorrhage.
  - **\> 80** for insertion/removal or epidural catheter.
  - **\> 100** for multi-trauma, TBI, ICH, neurosurgery, or posterior ophthalmic surgery.
- **Considerations:**
  - ABO/RhD matched where possible, but not essential.
  - Poor response may occur with sepsis, DIC, alloimmunization.

## Fresh Frozen Plasma (FFP)
- Contains all soluble coagulation factors including fibrinogen.
- Used in bleeding associated with coagulation factor deficiencies, massive hemorrhage, DIC.
- **Usual dose: 12–15 mL/kg (3–5 units)** (each unit contains 180–340 mL).
- **Considerations:**
  - Requires thawing (20-30 min).
  - Must be ABO compatible. Crossmatch not required.

## Cryoprecipitate
- Concentrated fibrinogen, factor VIII, factor XIII, vWF, fibronectin.
- Used in hypofibrinogenemia associated with bleeding.
- **Usual dose: 1 unit per 30 kg** (each unit contains 80–120 mL) → typically increases fibrinogen by ~1.0 g/L in a 70 kg adult.
- **Target fibrinogen (g/L):**
  - **≥1.5–2.0** during major bleeding.
  - **≥ 2.0** in obstetric hemorrhage.
- **Considerations:**
  - Requires thawing (~15 min).
  - Check fibrinogen level 30–60 min after transfusion.

## Beriplex (4-factor PCC)
- Contains factors II, VII, IX, X, protein C and S.
- Used for urgent warfarin reversal in major bleeding or emergency surgery, or acquired coagulopathy of major hemorrhage/trauma.
- **Usual dose: 25–50 IU/kg (max 5000 IU)** according to INR and indication.
- **Considerations:**
  - IV injection rate > 8 mL/min.
  - No repeat dosing within 14 days unless approved by NZBS.
  - Contains heparin.


## Albumin
- Plasma-derived colloid.
- Used for plasma volume expansion, hypoalbuminemia, or large volume paracentesis.
- **Formulations:**
  - 5% (50 g/L), 500 mL per vial.
  - 20% (200 g/L), 100 mL per vial.
- **Considerations:**
  - Not routinely indicated solely for correcting hypoalbuminemia.
  - Not recommended in TBI.


## Resources
- {{< badge content="Educational Resource" class="badge-lime" >}} [NZ Blood Transfusion Medicine Handbook](https://www.nzblood.co.nz/healthcare-professionals/transfusion-medicine/transfusion-medicine-handbook)
- **Red Cell Transfusion Thresholds:**
  - {{< badge content="Systematic Review / Meta-analysis" color="amber" >}} [Cochrane Review (2025):](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41114449/) Supports restrictive transfusion strategies for most patients. Exceptions include acute brain injury and acute MI.
  - {{< badge content="Key Trial" color="blue" >}} [SAHARA Trial (2024):](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39655786/) In aneurysmal SAH, a liberal transfusion threshold (Hb ≤ 100 g/L) did not significantly reduce unfavorable neurological outcomes at 12 months compared to a restrictive transfusion threshold (Hb ≤ 80 g/L).
  - {{< badge content="Key Trial" color="blue" >}} [TRAIN Trial (2024):](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39382241/) In acute brain injury (TBI, SAH, ICH), a liberal transfusion threshold (Hb < 90 g/L) reduced unfavorable neurological outcomes at 180 days compared to a restrictive transfusion threshold (Hb < 70 g/L).
  - {{< badge content="Key Trial" color="blue" >}} [HEMOTION Trial:](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38869931/) In moderate/severe TBI, a liberal transfusion threshold (Hb ≤ 100 g/L) showed a non-significant reduction in unfavorable neurological outcomes at 6 months, compared to a restrictive transfusion threshold (Hb ≤ 70 g/L).
  - {{< badge content="Key Trial" color="blue" >}} [TRICC Trial (1999):](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9971864/) In critically ill adults, a restrictive transfusion threshold (Hb < 70 g/L) showed no significant difference in 30-day mortality compared to a liberal transfusion threshold (Hb < 100 g/L).
  - {{< badge content="Key Trial" color="blue" >}} [TRICS III Trial (2017):](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29130845/) In cardiac surgery patients, a restrictive transfusion threshold (Hb < 75 g/L) was non-inferior to a liberal transfusion threshold (Hb < 95 g/L in OT/ICU, < 85 g/L on ward) for death, MI, stroke, renal failure at 28 days.
  - {{< badge content="Key Trial" color="blue" >}} [MINT Trial (2023):](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37952133/) In acute MI, a liberal transfusion (Hb < 100 g/L) showed a non-significant reduction in 30 day mortality or recurrent MI compared to a restrictive transfusion threshold (Hb < 70–80 g/L).
- **Plasma Transfusion:**
  - {{< badge content="Key Trial" color="blue" >}} [PROPPR Trial (2015):](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25647203/) In severe traumatic hemorrhage, a 1:1:1 plasma:platelet:RBC strategy did not significantly reduce 24 h or 30 day mortality, compared to 1:1:2.
