## Overview
VTE prophylaxis is recommended for most critically ill patients due to increased thrombotic risk from immobility, inflammation, and hypercoagulability. Options include pharmacologic and mechanical prophylaxis.

## VTE Prophylaxis Options

### Pharmacologic
- **Low molecular weight heparin (LMWH).**
  - Enoxaparin 40 mg subcut daily.
  - In severe renal dysfunction (CrCl <30 mL/min) → lower dose: 20 mg daily.
  - Adjust dosing for extremes of body weight and selected trauma patients.
- **Unfractionated heparin.**
  - Heparin 5000 units subcut q8–12h.
  - Alternative in severe renal impairment or when shorter anticoagulant duration is desirable.

> ⚠️ Contraindications include platelets < 50 × 10⁹/L, active bleeding, active HIT, significant coagulopathy with high bleeding risk, severe renal impairment.

### Mechanical
- **Sequential Compression Device (SCD):** 
  - Reduces venous stasis by intermittently compressing the lower limbs.
  - Preferred mechanical method.
  - Preferred when pharmacologic prophylaxis is contraindicated.
- **Graduated compression stockings:**
  - Alternative when SCDs unavailable or unsuitable.
  - Evidence is weaker, use is limited by skin injury risk.


## Specific Populations

| Population | VTE prophylaxis | Timing / considerations |
|---|---|---|
| **Medical ICU** | LMWH preferred; SCD if contraindicated | Initiate early if bleeding risk permits |
| **General surgical ICU** | LMWH preferred; SCD if contraindicated | Start once hemostasis is achieved; discuss with surgical team |
| **TBI with intracranial hemorrhage** | SCD initially → LMWH when safe | Repeat CT at ~24 h. Consider LMWH at 24–48 h if hemorrhage is stable, following neurosurgical discussion. Higher-risk injuries may require ≥72 h |
| **Spontaneous intracerebral hemorrhage** | SCD initially → LMWH when safe | Consider pharmacologic prophylaxis at 24–48 h if hemorrhage is stable, following neurosurgical discussion. |
| **Pelvic fractures / major trauma** | LMWH preferred; SCD if contraindicated | Initiate LMWH as early as safely possible once hemorrhage is controlled, often within 24–48 h |

> ⚠️ Routine prophylactic insertion of IVC filters are not recommended. Consider a retrievable filter in selected patients with established proximal DVT or PE when therapeutic anticoagulation is contraindicated.

## Resources
- {{< badge content="Guideline" color="green" >}} [ACS guidelines on TBI (2024):](https://www.facs.org/media/vgfgjpfk/best-practices-guidelines-traumatic-brain-injury.pdf) Recommends VTE prophylaxis within 24–48 h if hemorrhage is stable.
- {{< badge content="Guideline" color="green" >}} [AHA/ASA guideline on ICH (2022):](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35579034/) Grade 2B recommendation on considering initiating pharmacological DVT prophylaxis at 24–48 h after spontaneous ICH.
- {{< badge content="Guideline" color="green" >}} [ERATIC guidelines (2025):](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40696568/) Recommend against routine IVC filters for primary prevention of VTE.
- {{< badge content="Key Trial" color="blue" >}} [PREVENT trial (2019):](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30779530/) RCT where there was no demonstrable benefit from combining SCDs with pharmacological prophylaxis.
