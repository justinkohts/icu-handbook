## Overview

- Mechanical ventilation aims to provide adequate oxygenation and ventilation while minimizing ventilator-induced lung injury.
- There is no single best mode of mechanical ventilation for all patients.

{{< concept-card title="Main principles and typical targets" >}}
- **SpO₂:** 92–96%; or 88–92% in patients at risk of hypercapnic respiratory failure.
- **Tidal volumes:** 6 mL/kg predicted body weight.
- **Respiratory rate:** 10–25/min, adjusted to PaCO₂ targets.
  - Normocapnia or mild hypocapnia may be required in TBI.
  - Permissive hypercapnia may be required in ARDS.
- **Plateau pressure:** < 30 cmH₂O.
- **Driving pressure:** ideally < 15 cmH₂O.
- **PEEP:** begin at 5 cmH₂O. May require more if respiratory mechanics impaired. Consider following the [ARDSNet PEEP/FiO2 escalation table](/images/systems/airway-and-breathing/ardsnet-peep-table.png).
- ⚠️ Positive pressure ventilation may exacerbate or induce hypotension by ↑ intrathoracic pressure and ↓ venous return.
{{< /concept-card >}}




## Ventilator Modes
There are many modes of mechanical ventilation. Broadly speaking, they can be categorized into:
- **Controlled/mandatory:** volume control, pressure control.
- **Supported:** CPAP, pressure support.
- **Adaptive:** PRVC.
- *Note:* SIMV is a mixed controlled & supported mode.



{{< concept-cards >}}

{{< concept-card title="Volume-controlled ventilation" >}}
- A set **tidal volume** is delivered with each mandatory breath.
- **Airway pressure varies** with compliance, resistance, flow, and patient effort.
- Provides predictable tidal volume and minute ventilation.
- Airway pressures may increase if compliance worsens or resistance increases.
{{< /concept-card >}}


{{< concept-card title="Presssure-controlled ventilation" >}}
- A set **inspiratory pressure** is applied for a set inspiratory time.
- **Tidal volume varies** with compliance, resistance, and patient effort.
- Inspiratory flow is usually decelerating.
- Limits applied inspiratory pressure, can provide high initial inspiratory flow.
- Tidal volumes may fall if compliance worsens or resistance increases.
{{< /concept-card >}}

{{< concept-card title="Pressure support ventilation" >}}
- All breaths are **patient triggered**.
- Ventilator provides a preset level of inspiratory pressure, when triggered.
- Tidal volume depends on pressure support, patient effort, compliance, and resistance.
- Mechanical inspiration usually terminated when inspiratory gas flow falls to ~25% of initial flow rate.
- Trigger sensitivity, rise time, and cycling criteria can be adjusted to improve synchrony.
{{< /concept-card >}}

{{< concept-card title="SIMV" >}}
- Delivers a set number of **synchronized mandatory breaths**.
- Mandatory breaths may be volume or pressure controlled.
- Allows spontaneous breaths between mandatory breaths, usually with pressure support.

{{< /concept-card >}}


{{< concept-card title="Adaptive pressure control / PRVC type modes" >}}
- Delivers pressure-controlled breaths to achieve a target tidal volume.
- Inspiratory pressure is adjusted automatically from breath to breath.

{{< /concept-card >}}

{{< concept-card title="Adaptive Support Ventilation (ASV)" >}}
- Adaptive mode available on Hamilton ventilators.
- Targets a preset minute volume by **automatically** adjusting respiratory rate, tidal volume, and inspiratory pressure.
- Employs lung-protective strategies to minimize complications from Auto-PEEP and volutrauma/barotrauma.
{{< /concept-card >}}

{{< /concept-cards >}}


## Ventilator Settings
There are many ventilator settings. Common settings include the following.
- **Tidal volume:**
  - Determines volume delivered with each breath.
  - Lung protective range is 4–8 ml/kg PBW (predicted body weight).
  - Excessively large tidal volumes can result in ventilator-induced lung injury. 
- **Respiratory rate:**
  - In an adult, usually 8–25/min.
  - Together with tidal volume, determines minute ventilation and therefore influences PaCO₂.
  - High rates can cause incomplete expiration and auto-PEEP, particularly in asthma and COPD.
- **FiO₂:**
  - Adjust FiO₂ to achieve adequate oxygenation while avoiding unnecessary hyperoxia.
  - Oxygen targets should be individualized — a common target is SpO₂ 92–96%.
- **PEEP:**
  - Maintains positive airway pressure at end-expiration.
  - Usual range 5–15 cmH₂O.
  - Helps prevent alveolar collapse, improve alveolar recruitment, and improve oxygenation.
  - May also help in LV dysfunction due to ↓ LV afterload.
  - Excessive PEEP may cause alveolar distension, ↑ dead space, ↓ venous return, ↓ cardiac output, ↑ RV afterload.
- **Airway pressures:**
  - Peak pressure reflects resistive pressure + elastic pressure + PEEP.
  - Plateau pressure better reflects alveolar pressure.
  - In ARDS, aim for plateau pressure < 30 cmH₂O to ↓ barotrauma.

More advanced settings include:
- **Driving pressure (ΔP):**
  - ΔP = plateau pressure − PEEP
  - Represents the pressure required to deliver the tidal volume to the respiratory system.
  - Particularly in ARDS, higher values are associated with ↑ mechanical stress.
- **Inspiratory time and flow:**
  - These determine how quickly and for how long each breath is delivered.
  - Longer expiratory time is particularly important in obstructive lung disease (asthma, COPD) to reduce air trapping.

## Problems and Troubleshooting
- **Patient-ventilator dyssynchrony:** mismatch between the patient's respiratory effort and ventilator-delivered breaths.
  - Common causes include inadequate flow, inappropriate trigger sensitivity, inadequate support, pain/anxiety, excessive respiratory drive.
- **Flow starvation:** inspiratory flow insufficient for the patient's demand.
  - Produces a scooped/concave pressure waveform, most commonly seen in volume controlled ventilation.
  - Causes discomfort, ↑ work of breathing, dyssynchrony, excessive negative pressure (↑ risk of micro-aspiration and hemodynamic disturbances).
  - Treat by ↑ inspiratory flow, adjusting flow pattern, ↑ support, or treating cause of increased respiratory drive.
- **High airway pressure:**
  - **↑ resistance:** secretions, mucus plugging, kinked ETT, bronchospasm.
  - **↓ compliance:** ARDS, pulmonary edema, atelectasis, pneumothorax.
  - Differentiate the two by assessing the peak vs plateau pressure.
- **Auto-PEEP / dynamic hyperinflation:** due to incomplete expiration before the next breath begins.
  - Expiratory flow does not return to zero before next breath.
  - Treat by ↓ RR, ↓ tidal volume, ↑ expiratory time, and treating airflow obstruction.

## Risks and complications
Complications of invasive mechanical ventilation can be categorized into:
- **Airway:** laryngeal edema, oral/pharyngeal injury, vocal cord dysfunction.
- **Pulmonary:** ventilator-induced lung injury, atelectasis, dyssynchrony.
- **Cardiovascular:** ↓ venous return, ↓ cardiac output, RV dysfunction, ↓ splanchnic/renal blood flow.
- **Infection:** ventilator-associated pneumonia ([risk accumulates over time](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9735080/)), airway colonization. 
- **Muscle:** diaphragmatic atropy, ICU-acquired weakness.
- **Brain:** sedation, delirium, sleep disturbance.
