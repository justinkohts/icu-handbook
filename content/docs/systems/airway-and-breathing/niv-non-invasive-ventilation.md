## Overview
- Non-Invasive Ventilation (NIV) provides positive-pressure respiratory support without an ETT, usually through a tight-fitting face mask.
- NIV can improve gas exchange, work of breathing, and avoid complications associated with invasive mechanical ventilation.
- Acute NIV should be commenced in an appropriately-monitored environment with trained staff and ability to escalate rapidly to invasive ventilation.


## Modes

{{< concept-cards >}}

{{< concept-card title="CPAP (Continuous Positive Airway Pressure)" >}}
- Delivers a single constant pressure throughout inspiration and expiration.
- **Does not provide inspiratory pressure support** (therefore does not augment tidal volume).
- ⚠️ strictly, CPAP is not a 'ventilation' mode.
- Used in type 1 respiratory failure.
- **Typical settings:** 5–10 cmH₂O.
- **Mechanism:** alveolar recruitment, ↑ FRC, improved V/Q mismatch, ↑ respiratory compliance, ↓ LV afterload.
{{< /concept-card >}}


{{< concept-card title="BiPAP (Bilevel/Biphasic Positive Airway Pressure)" >}}
- Delivers:
  - **IPAP:** inspiratory positive airway pressure.
  - **EPAP:** expiratory positive airway pressure, analogous to PEEP.
- ***Pressure support = IPAP − EPAP***
  - Pressure support increases tidal volume and alveolar ventilation.
- Used in type 2 respiratory failure.
- **Typical settings:** start with EPAP 3–5 cmH₂O, IPAP 10–15 cmH₂O. Increase IPAP by 2–5 cmH₂O every 10 minutes, until a target IPAP of 20–30 cmH₂O or as tolerated.
- **Mechanism:** CO₂ clearance, respiratory muscle unloading, overcomes dynamic airway collapse.
{{< /concept-card >}}

{{< /concept-cards >}}


## Indications
- **CPAP:**
  - Acute cardiogenic pulmonary edema.
  - Acute hypoxemic (type 1) respiratory failure.
  - Post-operative hypoxemia due to atelectasis.
  - Obstructive sleep apnea.
- **Bilevel/BiPAP/NIV:**
  - Acute hypercapnic (type 2) respiratory failure, particularly AECOPD.
  - Acute-on-chronic ventilatory failure e.g. neuromuscular disease, restrictive lung disease.


## Contraindications
- Reduced level of consciousness, impaired cough, bulbar dysfunction.
- Hemodynamic instability.
- Bowel obstruction, upper GI hemorrhage, active vomiting (aspiration risk).
- Undrained pneumothorax.
- Agitation.
- Facial trauma.


## Monitoring
**Before starting NIV:**
- Identify and treat underlying cause.
- Establish the ceiling of treatment and escalation plan (e.g. tracheal intubation).

**After starting NIV:**
- Continuously assess clinical status (RR, work of breathing, consciousness, SpO2).
- Repeat blood gas assessment.
- An improving trajectory should become apparent within 1–2 hours.


### NIV Failure
NIV failure should trigger active reassessment. Troubleshoot the mask/interface problems, optimize the NIV pressures, and when indicated, proceed to intubation.

Features suggesting NIV failure:
- ↑ RR, work of breathing.
- Worsening hypercapnia / acidosis.
- Worsening hypoxemia.
- Hemodynamic instability.
- Deteriorating consciousness.
- Mask intolerance or uncontrollable leak.
- Inability to clear secretions.


## Risks and Complications
- Delayed intubation.
- Pressure injury to face / nasal bridge.
- Mask intolerance and ↑ anxiety.
- Gastric insufflation, aspiration pneumonia.
- Hypotension from ↓ venous return.
