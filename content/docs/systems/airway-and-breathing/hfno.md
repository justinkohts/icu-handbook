## Overview
High Flow Nasal Cannula Oxygen (HFNO) delivers a heated, humidified air-oxygen mixture via wide-bore nasal cannulae.

Adult systems generally provide:
- **Flow:** up to approximately 60 L/min.
- **FiO₂:** 0.21–1.0.

Flow and FiO₂ can be adjusted independently.


## Mechanism

HFNO provides multiple physiological benefits:

- **More reliable FiO₂ delivery** by matching/exceeding peak inspiratory flow.
- **Nasopharyngeal dead space washout**,reducing CO₂ rebreathing.
- **Generates low-level positive airway pressure**, particularly with the mouth closed.
- **Heated humidification** and **improved secretion clearance**.
- **May reduce work of breathing**.


## Indications

- Acute hypoxemic respiratory failure.
- Post-extubation oxygen support in selected patients.
- Preoxygenation and apneic oxygenation during intubation.
- Mask relief / breaks from NIV.
- Selected procedures such as bronchoscopy or procedural sedation.


## Contraindications

- Blocked nasal passages.
- Unprotected airway.
- Significant facial/nasal trauma.
- Base of skull fracture.
- Severe epistaxis.


## Management

- Use appropriately sized cannulae which do not completely occlude the nares.
- **Flow:** commonly 40–60 L/min, adjusted to comfort and work of breathing.
  - Increasing flow improves inspiratory flow matching, dead space washout, airway pressure, and work of breathing.
- **FiO₂:** titrate to target SpO₂.
  - Increasing FiO₂ mainly improves oxygenation.


## Complications

- Nasal discomfort, mucosal trauma, epistaxis.
- Gastric distension.
- Barotrauma, pneumothorax.
