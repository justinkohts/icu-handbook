## Overview
An infection screen aims to identify the **source** of infection and **causative organism** in a patient with suspected infection or sepsis. Investigations should be directed by the suspected source of infection.

**Principles:**
- Obtain microbiological specimens before antibiotics where feasible without delaying urgent treatment.
- Distinguish infection from colonization or contamination, particularly in patients with invasive devices (e.g. ETT, CVC).
- Use targeted imaging to identify infections requiring source control.


## Blood Cultures
- **2 sets**, each containing an aerobic and anaerobic bottles (8–10 mL each bottle).
- Peripheral venipuncture preferred.
- If catether-related bloodstream infection is suspected, obtain paired peripheral and line cultures with appropriate labeling.
- **Differential time to positivity** ≥ 2 hours (line cultures positive earlier) may aid discrimination between catheter-related infection or bloodstream infection.
> ⚠️ Avoid unnecessary repeat blood cultures, except when clearance must be documented, e.g. S. aureus bacteremia or persistent/endovascular infection.


## Urine Microscopy and Cultures
Catheter-associated bacteriuria and pyuria are common in ICU patients and do not necessarily indicate infection.

- **CAUTI:** Compatible clinical features and urine culture ≥10³ CFU/mL, without a more likely source.
- **Asymptomatic bacteriuria:** Generally does not require treatment, except in selected circumstances (e.g., pregnancy).
- If CAUTI is suspected and the catheter has been in place for >2 weeks, replace it if still required and obtain urine from the new catheter.


## Respiratory Cultures
In suspected pneumonia, obtain a respiratory specimen for microscopy and culture. Results must be interpreted alongside clinical findings because airway colonization is common, particularly in the presence of an artificial airway.


| Method | Description | Indication | Limitations |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Expectorated sputum** | Patient coughs up lower respiratory secretions into sterile container | Initial sampling in nonintubated patients with suspected pneumonia | Salivary contamination<br>Variable sample quality |
| **Induced sputum** | Nebulized hypertonic saline stimulates coughing & sputum production | Patients unable to expectorate<br>Particularly useful for TB & PJP | Aerosol generation<br>Bronchospasm<br>Variable diagnostic yield |
| **Endotracheal aspirate** | Sterile suction catheter passed through ETT to collect secretions | Preferred initial test for suspected VAP | Airway colonization reduces specificity |
| **Bronchial washings** | Sterile saline instilled and aspirated through a bronchoscope **without wedging** into a bronchial segment |  Bronchoscopic sampling of proximal airway secretions | Proximal airway contamination and colonization reduce specificity<br>Less representative of distal infection |
| **BAL** | Bronchoscope wedged into a bronchial segment; sterile saline instilled & aspirated | Suspected opportunistic infection, nonresolving pneumonia, or unexplained pulmonary infiltrates | Invasive, risks hypoxemia and bronchospasm |


## Lumbar Puncture
In suspected meningitis or encephalitis, send CSF cell count and differential, protein, glucose with paired blood glucose, Gram stain, culture, and pathogen-specific PCR as indicated.

**Typical patterns** for CSF infection are listed below. CSF findings may overlap and confirmatory diagnosis by culture or PCR might take time. Do not delay empiric treatment for LP.

| Parameter | Normal | Bacterial | Viral | TB | Fungal |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Appearance** | Clear | Cloudy/turbid | Clear | Fibrin web | Clear/cloudy |
| **WBC (cells ×10⁶/L)** | 0–5, lymphocytes | >1000, predominantly neutrophils | 5–1000, predominantly lymphocytes | 10–500, predominantly lymphocytes | 0–500, predominantly lymphocytes |
| **Protein (g/L)** | 0.15–0.45 | ↑↑ (>1) | Normal/↑ (<1) | ↑↑ (1–5) | Normal/↑ (0.4–2.5) |
| **CSF:serum glucose ratio** | >0.6 | ↓ (<0.4) | Normal (>0.6) | ↓ (<0.4) | Normal/↓ (<0.4 when reduced) |
| **Opening pressure (cmH₂O)** | 10–20 | ↑ | Normal/↑ | ↑ | ↑ to ↑↑ |




## Ascitic Fluid Sampling
**Diagnostic paracentesis** is indicated in acutely unwell patients with cirrhosis and ascites, particularly when infection is suspected.

**Spontaneous bacterial peritonitis:**
- Ascitic PMNs (usually neutrophils) ≥ 250 cells/mm³.
- Send cell count, differential, and culture.
- Using blood culture bottles when sampling improves culture yield.

**Secondary bacterial peritonitis:**
- Suspect with polymicrobial cultures, abdominal signs, markedly elevated PMN counts, or poor response to treatment.
- Runyon criteria (≥2 of 3):
  - Protein >10 g/L.
  - Glucose <2.8 mmol/L.
  - LDH >serum upper limit of normal.
