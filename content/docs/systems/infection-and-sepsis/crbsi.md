## Overview
**Catheter-Related Bloodstream Infections (CRBSIs)** are a clinical diagnosis of bloodstream infection attributable to an intravascular catheter, supported by microbiological evidence. Common pathogens include coagulase-negative staphylococci, staphylococcus aureus, enterococci, gram-negative bacilli, and candida.

It is no longer common practice to routinely remove or replace CVLs; only when infected or no longer required. 


## Prevention
### CVL Insertion Bundle
- Appropriate catheter indication and site selection.
  - Subclavian access generally has lower infection risk, but balance against pneumothorax, bleeding, and thrombosis.
- Hand hygiene and maximal sterile barrier precautions.
- Skin antiseptic using 2% chlorhexidine in 70% isopropyl alcohol, allowing complete drying.
- Ultrasound-guided insertion using sterile technique.
- Insertion checklist.


### CVL Maintenance Bundle
- Daily review of catheter necessity and prompt removal when unnecessary.
- Daily review of entry site for inflammation.
- Dedicated access for parenteral nutrition.
- Asepsis when accessing CVL lumens.


## Clinical Presentation
CRBSI should be suspected in any patient with a CVL present for > 48 h who develops:
- Fever
- Inflammation or purulence at the insertion site
- Increasing WCC or inflammatory markers
- Hemodynamic instability
- Altered mental status
- Positive blood culture with likely organism and no other likely source of infection 

> ⚠️ Absence of local inflammation does not exclude CRBSI.


## Diagnosis
- **Obtain ≥2 blood culture sets** before antibiotics, ideally paired peripheral and catheter samples collected simultaneously with equivalent blood volumes. Label sampling sites and consider culturing individual lumens of multilumen catheters.
- Microbiological criteria supporting CRBSI:
  1. **Quantitative cultures:** Central line sample has colony count ≥3 times peripheral count.
  2. **Differential time to positivity:** Central line sample becomes culture positive ≥2 hours before peripheral culture, with the same organism.
  3. **Catheter tip culture** (after CVL removal): Same organism isolated from catheter tip and peripheral blood


## Management
### Catheter Removal
- **Prompt removal** is indicated in:
  - Confirmed CRBSI.
  - Suspected infection with signs of severity and no other sources.
  - Unexplained fever.
- **Catheter salvage** may be considered for essential long-term catheters (e.g. surgically implanted catheters) particularly in clinically stable patients without complicated infection. Systemic antibiotics ± antibiotic lock therapy may be used. Discuss with Infectious Diseases.


### Antimicrobial Therapy
#### Choice of Antimicrobials
- Obtain blood cultures **before** initiating empiric IV antibiotics when feasible.
- Cover **gram-positive organisms** (including MRSA where indicated) e.g. with Vancomycin.
- **Antipseudomonal** therapy includes Tazocin and Meropenem.


#### Treatment Duration
- Depends on the organism, ability for source control, and complications. Discuss with Infectious Diseases.
- Broadly speaking:
  - **Uncomplicated CRBSI after removal:** 7 days for most organisms, 14 daays for S.aureus or Candida.
  - **Complicated CRBSI:** 4–6 weeks for endocarditis, septic metastases, osteomyelitis.
  - **Line colonization** (line culture positive, peripheral blood cultures negative): 3–5 days. No antibiotics may be required for CoNS, enterococci, or enterobacteriaceae.
