## Overview
Infection control precautions aim to prevent transmission of an infectious organism between patients, healthcare workers, and the environment. Critically ill patients are particularly vulnerable to healthcare-associated infections due to invasive devices, impaired host defenses, and prolonged hospitalization.


## Standard Precautions
Standard precautions are the minimum infection prevention measures applied to all patients. They assume that blood, body fluids, nonintact skin, mucous membranes, and potentially contaminated equipment may transmit infectious organisms.

### Hand Hygiene
Hand hygiene is one of the most important interventions for preventing healthcare-associated infections. [Hand Hygiene New Zealand](https://tewhatuora.shinyapps.io/Hand-Hygiene-NZ-dashboard/) has set a target of 80% compliance to the [WHO Five Moments of Hand Hygiene](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241597906).

**Wash your hands:**
1. Before touching a patient.
2. Before a clean or aseptic procedure.
3. After body fluid exposure risk.
4. After touching a patient.
5. After touching patient surroundings.

**Hand hygiene practice:**
- **Alcohol-based hand rub:** Preferred when hands are not visibly soiled.
- **Soap and water:** Required when visibly soiled; preferred for suspected or confirmed C. difficile according to local policy.
- Gloves do not replace hand hygiene.
- Keep sleeves above the elbow, nails short, avoid artificial nails, and remove jewelry that interferes with hand hygiene.

### Other Standard Precautions
- **PPE:** Gloves, gowns/aprons, masks, eye protection according to anticipated exposure.
- **Aseptic technique:** For handling sterile procedures/equipment.
- **Sharps safety:** Disposal into sharps containers.
- **Environmental cleaning:** Regular disinfection of frequently touched surfaces/shared equipment.


## Transmission-Based Precautions
Additional precautions are required for patients with known or suspected transmissible infections. Some of these infections require combined precautions.

| Precaution | Examples | Principal measures |
|---|---|---|
| **Contact** | C. difficile, VRE, select MDROs | Gloves & gown/apron, dedicated equipment, isolation |
| **Droplet** | Influenza, pertussis, meningococcal disease | Medical mask, appropriate eye protection |
| **Airborne** | Tuberculosis, measles, varicella | N95 mask, negative pressure isolation room |


## Prevention of Healthcare-Associated Infections (HAIs)
**Invasive devices** increase the risk of infection by disrupting normal anatomical barriers and providing surfaces for microbial colonization. **Prevention** of such HAIs require aseptic insertion, appropriate care/maintenance, and early device removal.

| Infection | Key prevention strategies |
|---|---|
| **CLABSI/CRBSI** | Sterile barriers, chlorhexidine skin antiseptic, aseptic line access, appropriate dressings, daily review of line necessity |
| **VAP** | Head of bed elevation 30–45°, minimize sedation, daily extubation assessment, oral care, toothbrushing |
| **CAUTI** | Avoid unnecessary catheterization, aseptic insertion, early removal |
