## Nutrition
Severe burns produce a sustained hypermetabolic and catabolic response, mediated by catecholamines, cortisol, glucagon, and inflammatory cytokines. Early enteral nutrition (either gastric or post-pyloric) should ideally commence within 12–24 hours of injury, when clinically appropriate. Seek dietician advice early.

Nutritional requirements:
- **Protein:** 1.5–2 g/kg/day in adults; 1.5–3 g/kg/day in children.
- **Energy requirements:** preferably determined using indirect calorimetry to avoid underfeeding or overfeeding. In the absence of indirect calorimetry, the Toronto formula may be used.

## Micronutrient Supplementation
Trace elements are lost through burn exudate, increasing requirements for wound healing. Consider measuring zinc, copper, and selenium, with supplementation guided by specialist dietician advice.

Micronutrient supplmentation generally includes:
- Vitamin C: 500 mg PO/NG twice daily.
- Elemental zinc: 50 mg PO/NG daily.
- Multivitamin: 2 tablets PO/NG daily.
- Folic acid: 5 mg PO/NG daily.
