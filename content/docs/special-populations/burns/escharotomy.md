## Overview
An **escharotomy** is a decompressive incision through the inelastic eschar formed by partial thickness or full-thickness burns. Deep burns and progressive edema (exacerbated by fluid resuscitation) increases pressure beneath the rigid eschar, potentially compromising the airway, ventilation, or limb perfusion. This procedure is usually performed by Plastics/Burns surgeons.

## Indications
Consider escharotomy in deep dermal or full thickness **circumferential burns**, with actual or anticipated compromise. **Prophylactic escharotomy** may be considered in selected high-risk patients, particularly before interhospital transfer.

| Escharotomy Location | Indications |
|---|---|
| **Limbs** | Increasing pain/tightness, coolness, pallor, delayed capillary refill, diminished pulses |
| **Chest / abdomen** | Restricted chest expansion, reduced compliance, impaired ventilation |
| **Neck** | Threatened airway due to constricting eschar |

## Escharotomy Sites
Standard escharotomy incisions are in green. These avoid superficial neurovascular structures.

## Monitoring
Following escharotomy, reassess distal perfusion and ventilation with **hourly neurovascular observations**. Persistent ischemia despite adequate eschar decompression should prompt reassessment for **compartment syndrome** and possible fasciotomy.
