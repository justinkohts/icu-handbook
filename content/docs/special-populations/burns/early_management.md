## First Aid
- **Stop the burning process:** remove patient from source, remove clothing and jewelry (unless adherent).
- **Cooling:** cool the burn with running water for 20 mins, immediately or within 3 hours of injury.
	- Water temprature 8–25°C (aim approximately 15°C).
	- Avoid ice or ice-cold water.
	- **Prevent hypothermia.** Use active warming as required.
- **Analgesia.**
- **Wound covering:** loosely apply cling film lengthwise as a temporary dressing. Avoid circumferential wrapping.
- **Tetanus prophylaxis.**

> ⚠️ **Chemical burns** require copious irrigation for > 30 mins. **Electrical burns** require consideration of potential compartment syndrome, cardiac dysrhythmias, muscle necrosis, and multi-organ involvement.

## Initial Stabilization
Continue the primary survey with frequent reassessment, as edema and physiological disturbances may evolve.

| Component | Assessment & Management |
|---|---|
| **Airway** | Assess patency, inhalation injury, and edema. Consider early intubation. Protect C-spine if indicated. |
| **Breathing** | Assess oxygenation, ventilation, and chest expansion. Administer supplemental O₂. |
| **Circulation** | Assess perfusion and hemorrhage. Establish 2 large-bore IV lines. Initiate fluid resuscitation if indicated. |
| **Disability** | Assess GCS, pupils, and glucose. Consider trauma, hypoxia, or toxic exposure if altered consciousness. Administer analgesia as required. |
| **Exposure** | Examine burns and associated injuries. Remove clothing/jewelry. Prevent hypothermia (Bair hugger, warm room). |

> ⚠️ Opioids in large quantities may be required for analgesia.


## Fluid Resuscitation
Due to increased vascular permeability and edema formation, patients with severe burns are prone to hypovolemia and subsequent circulatory shock. Fluid resuscitation is required in **adults with > 20% TBSA** burns and **children with > 10% TBSA** burns.

Use the **modified Parkland formula** to calculate fluid requirements:

$$
\text{Fluid volume (mL)} = 3 × \%\text{TBSA} × \text{Weight (kg)}
$$

- Administer **50% in the first 8 hours** (from time of burn injury) and **50% over the following 16 hours**.
- Use balanced crystalloid (Hartmann's, Plasmalyte).
- Children < 16 years require additional glucose-containing maintenance fluid **in addition** to resuscitation fluid. Examples include 5% Dextrose with Plasmalyte, or 5% Dextrose with NaCl 0.9%.
- Albumin 5% can be considered **after the first 18-24h**, for very large burns, inhalation injury, or large pediatric burns. The Modified Brooke formula describes 0.3–0.5 mL/kg/%TBSA over 24h.
- Be mindful of fluid creep (excessive volume loading).
- Monitor for signs of rhabdomyolysis (myoglobinuria is cola/tea-colored urine). A higher urine output target may be required.

#### Burn Resuscitation Endpoints

| Endpoint | Target Range |
|---|---|
| **Urine output** | Adults: 0.5 mL/kg/h<br>Children: 0.5–1 mL/kg/h<br>Infants: 1 mL/kg/h |
| **Mental status** | Follows commands; purposeful, age-appropriate activity |
| **Heart rate** | Adults: <130 bpm<br>Children: <140 bpm<br>Infants: <160 bpm |
| **Systolic BP** | Adults: >90 mmHg<br>Children: 70–90 + (2 × age in years) mmHg<br>Infants: 60–70 mmHg |
| **CVP** | 6–10 mmHg |
| **SVV** | <13% |
| **Lactate** | <2 mmol/L |
| **Base deficit** | Trend toward zero |
| **Hematocrit** | Gradual dilution |


## Burn Wound Management
### Initial Wound Care
- Provide adequate analgesia before wound assessment and dressing.
- Remove loose, non-viable skin and clean the wound according to the Burns/Plastics team's instructions.
- Blister management: leave small blisters intact. Release large tense blisters. Debride blisters over joints or those restricting movement.
- Cover debrided areas with an apporpriate dressing.

### Temporary Dressings for Transfer
- Discuss definitive wound management with the Burns/Plastics team.
- Broadly speaking, acceptable dressings depend on transfer time to the receiving burns unit:
  - < 8h → cling film is acceptable.
  - 8–24h → non-adherent primary layer against wound, with protective absorbent secondary dressing.
  - \> 24h → consider antimicrobial dressing after specialist consultation.
- Avoid circumferential or restrictive bandaging.
- Maintain normothermia throughout wound care and trasnfer.
