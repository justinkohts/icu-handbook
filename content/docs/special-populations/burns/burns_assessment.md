## Overview
Manage major burns using a **trauma-based approach** and a trauma assessment should be completed. Immediate life-threatening conditions, including airway compromise and associated traumatic injuries, take priority over the burn wound itself. **Reassess frequently**, as airway edema, circulatory compromise, and burn depth may evolve over time. The extent of the burn injury (calculated as %TBSA) guides management and prognosis.


## Primary Survey & Resuscitation
| Component | Assessment & Management |
|---|---|
| **Airway** | Assess patency, inhalation injury, and edema. Consider early intubation. Protect C-spine if indicated. |
| **Breathing** | Assess oxygenation, ventilation, and chest expansion. Administer supplemental O₂. |
| **Circulation** | Assess perfusion and hemorrhage. Establish 2 large-bore IV lines. Initiate fluid resuscitation if indicated. |
| **Disability** | Assess GCS, pupils, and glucose. Consider trauma, hypoxia, or toxic exposure if altered consciousness. Administer analgesia as required. |
| **Exposure** | Examine burns and associated injuries. Remove clothing/jewelry. Prevent hypothermia (Bair hugger, warm room). |

> ⚠️ Hypotension immediately after a burn is not necessarily caused by burn shock. Assess for hemorrhagic shock and other causes of circulatory shock.


## Burns Wound Assessment
### History
In a major burn, history-taking should occur alongside the primary survey without delaying airway management, treatment of associated trauma, or resuscitation.

- **Mechanism and events:** type of burn, intensity/duration of exposure, associated trauma or loss of consciousness.
  - **Electrical injury:** voltage, duration of contact, entry/exit sites, fall/cardiac arrest.
  - **Chemical injury:** agent, concentration, duration of exposure, whether decontamination has occurred.
- **Time & place:** time of injury, enclosed versus open space, environmental hazards.
- **Inhalation injury:** smoke exposure, airway symptoms, CO/cyanide exposure.
- **Unexplained injury:** inconsistent history/wound, delayed presentation, suspicious injury pattern (e.g. immersion scalds, symmetric distribution).
- **First aid & initial treatment:** cooling, decontamination, analgesia, fluids, dressings.
- **Patient factors:** comorbidities, pregnancy, medications, allergies, tetanus status, **weight**.


### Burn Size Estimation
The extent of burn injury, expressed as percentage total body surface area (**%TBSA**), is used to:
- Guide initial fluid resuscitation.
- Inform referral to a specialist burn center.
- Estimate injury severity and prognosis.

#### Lund-Browder Chart
The **Lund-Browder chart** is the preferred conventional method for estimating %TBSA, particularly in children because it accounts for age-related differences in body proportions. An initial estimate should be made during assessment and refined with the Burns/Plastics team.

- **Include all partial-thickness and full-thickness burns** when calculating %TBSA.
- **Exclude epidermal (superficial) burns**.

![Lund-Browder chart](/images/special-populations/burns/lund_browder.png)


#### Palmar Method
For scattered and patchy burns, the **palmar method** may be useful. Each patient's palm + digits ≈ 1% TBSA.

![Palmar method](/images/special-populations/burns/palmar.png)



### Burn Depth Estimation
Estimating burn depth guides wound management and predicts healing.
- **Superficial/epidermal burns** are typically moist, pink, painful, briskly blanching. Similar to sunburns. Exclude these from %TBSA estimation.
- **Partial thickness burns** have 3 subclassifications: superficial dermal, mid-dermal, and deep dermal. See table below for their characteristics.
- **Full thickness burns** are dry, leathery, non-blanching, insensate. Can have an overlying eschar. Color can vary from pale white to brown/black.

| Depth | Color of Dermis | Blisters | Capillary Refill | Sensation |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| **Epidermal** | Red | Epidermis damaged but intact (dry, no blisters) | Present – normal/brisk | Present |
| **Superficial Dermal** | Uniformly pale pink | Present – usually small and delayed (hours) | Present – normal/brisk | Painful |
| **Mid Dermal** | Dark pink or blotchy | Present – usually large and appear quickly | Sluggish | Variable |
| **Deep Dermal** | Blotchy red or fixed staining | Variable | Absent | Absent |
| **Full Thickness** | White, black, or charred | No | Absent | Absent |

### Burn Location and Distribution

- **Special anatomical areas:** face, hands, feet, perineum/genitalia, and major joints. These may result in significant functional or cosmetic impairment despite a small %TBSA.
- **Circumferential burns:**
  - **Limbs:** risk of impaired distal perfusion due to noncompliant eschar and progressive edema.
  - **Chest/torso:** risk of restricted chest expansion and ventilation.
  - These may necessitate escharotomy.
- **Inhalation injury:** consider associated airway and pulmonary injury, particularly in facial burns or enclosed-space fires.
- **Electrical burns:** cutaneous injury may underestimate deep tissue injury.
- **Chemical burns:** injury may progress until the chemical agent is removed or inactivated.
